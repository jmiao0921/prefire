    How it works

    Three questions.
    One prediction.

    A Decision Tree asks yes-or-no questions about the weather until it reaches a risk level. This tree learned its questions from 3,000 real days in BC.

    Trained model · 3,000 BC examples, 2010–2025

    What the model looks at.

    Four weather measurements, each one known to dry out forests or help fire spread.

    Air temperature

    °C, daily maximum

    Hot air dries out fuels like needles, grass and dead wood.

    Source: Open-Meteo (ERA5 for training, live forecast for today)

    Relative humidity

    %, daily minimum

    Dry air pulls moisture out of fuels so they catch fire easily.

    Source: Open-Meteo (ERA5 for training, live forecast for today)

    Wind speed

    km/h, daily maximum at 10 m

    Wind feeds oxygen to a fire and carries embers ahead of it.

    Source: Open-Meteo (ERA5 for training, live forecast for today)

    Rainfall

    mm, past 7 days

    Recent rain keeps fuels wet. A dry week lets them cure.

    Source: Open-Meteo (ERA5 for training, live forecast for today)

    How well it works.

    Tested on examples the tree never saw while learning, using 5-fold cross-validation: the data is split into 5 parts, and each part takes a turn as the hidden test.

    69.2%

    Accuracy

    Days it sorted correctly into "before a big fire" or "not". Guessing gets 50%.

    68.5%

    Big fires caught

    Of the days before a fire grew to 10 ha or more, how many the tree flagged.

    Rain

    Most important clue

    Rain over the past 7 days is the tree's first question. A dry week is the strongest warning sign.

    3

    Questions, at most

    Trees from 2 to 8 questions deep were tested. 3 scored best; deeper trees overfit.

    Science fair project

    Prefire.

    Predicting British Columbia wildfire risk to protect wildlife habitats.

    JM
    Jonathan Miao Grade 9 · David Thompson Secondary School · Vancouver, BC

    An AI-powered web tool using Decision Tree classification. It learns from historical air temperature, humidity, wind and rainfall to find the weather patterns that come before severe wildfires, then maps the areas of BC at risk so conservationists, researchers and authorities can act early.

    886,300 ha

    of BC forest burned in last year's wildfire season, along with the habitat it gave to the animals living there.

    Who lives there.

    Some species depend on forests that take decades or centuries to grow back.

    Woodland caribou

    Feed on lichen that grows in old forests. Lichen takes decades to return after a fire.

    Grizzly bear

    Need large, connected areas of habitat. Severe fires break up the ranges they travel through.

    Northern spotted owl

    One of Canada's most endangered birds. It needs old-growth forest that cannot be replaced quickly.

    The method.

    From raw records to a map anyone can read.

    1. CollectHistorical weather and wildfire records for BC.
    2. CleanMatch weather readings to fire locations and dates.
    3. TrainFit a Decision Tree on temperature, humidity, wind and rain.
    4. TestMeasure accuracy on examples the model has not seen.
    5. MapRun the model across BC and show the results here.
